## 1. Executive Summary & Audit Purpose

In logistics and freight financial management, terminology ambiguity creates operational friction and financial misinterpretation. A user viewing "Delivery Earnings", "Delivery Rate", "Gross Revenue", and "Revenue" across different views may assume they represent different calculations (e.g., net vs. gross, billed vs. collected, or company vs. sub-carrier rates). 

This comprehensive system-wide audit and standardization initiative establishes **single canonical terms** for every operational, financial, and fleet concept across RouteX. All screens—including Dashboard, Deliveries, Record Delivery, Delivery Details Drawer, Itemized Financial Breakdowns, Truck Profitability, Customizable Reports, and CSV Exports—now speak a unified language.

### Core Non-Negotiable Invariants Preserved
1. **Preserve Business Meaning & Calculations:** No financial formulas, profit equations, database schema identifiers, API wire payloads, or domain invariants were altered.
2. **Preserve Distinct Concepts:** Terms representing genuinely different concepts remain strictly distinct (e.g., *Delivery Revenue* is never conflated with *Net Profit*; *Fuel Rate* (₱/L) is never conflated with *Delivery Revenue* (₱ total)).
3. **Data Integrity for Incomplete Financials:** Missing or unknown expense items continue to trigger strict verification guards and are never silently coerced to zero.

## 3. Preserved Distinct Concepts

To avoid unintended domain or financial corruption, the audit verified that similar-sounding but fundamentally different terms are **strictly preserved**:

### A. Delivery Revenue vs. Net Profit
- **Delivery Revenue** represents gross inflow billed to the customer (contracted revenue).
- **Net Profit** represents revenue minus operating disbursements ($\text{Revenue} - \text{Expenses}$).
- Conflating them in UI labels or summaries would lead stakeholders to believe revenue is take-home profit.

### B. Fuel Rate vs. Delivery Revenue
- In `rrliRev/src/app/(workspace)/deliveries/new/page.tsx`, both `Fuel Rate` (diesel pump price, e.g. ₱85.00/L) and `Delivery Revenue` (billed rate, e.g. ₱28,000.00) exist.
- Both inputs were audited: `Fuel Rate` is labeled with `Fuel rate (₱ / L)` under **Vehicle & Route**, while `Delivery Revenue` is labeled under **Trip Revenue & Expenses**. Their conceptual and computational separation is rigorously preserved.

### C. Delivery Rate vs. Internal Database Field Names
- The database schema and TypeScript domain entities store the numeric rate as `deliveryRate: number`.
- Consistent with system rules, internal database columns, REST endpoints, and serialized JSON keys remain `deliveryRate`, while all user-facing labels, table headers, drawers, tooltips, and exports are presented as **Delivery Revenue**.

### D. Itemized Expense Categories vs. Consolidated Trip Expenses
- In the itemized breakdown, individual expense lines (*Diesel Fuel*, *Highway Tolls*, *Store Entry Fees*, *Crew Compensation*, *Other Expenses*) remain itemized and never lumped together as generic "Expenses".
- The consolidated sum is universally designated **Trip Expenses**.

## 4. Ambiguous Terms Evaluated & Resolved

During the audit, several ambiguous terms across legacy prototypes and new pages were identified, evaluated against business meaning, and resolved:

1. **"Total Delivery Earnings" vs. "Delivery Earnings" vs. "Gross Revenue"**
   - *Resolution:* Standardized to **Delivery Revenue**. The word "Earnings" implies net take-home pay for an individual driver, whereas in logistics, the company bills revenue to corporate clients (e.g., Landers, Vanguard).
2. **"Toll Gate Charges" vs. "Highway Tolls" vs. "Tolls" vs. "Toll Fees"**
   - *Resolution:* Standardized to **Highway Tolls**. In the Philippines, toll expressways (SLEX, NLEX, MCX, SCTEX, TPLEX, CALAX) are legally highway concessions. "Highway Tolls" is concise, professional, and clear.
3. **"Crew Payroll" vs. "Crew Costs" vs. "Crew Compensation"**
   - *Resolution:* Standardized to **Crew Compensation**. RouteX does not function as a human resources payroll system (which issues formal tax slips, SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG deductions, and monthly salary). Instead, it captures daily trip rates and per-diem trip allowances. "Crew Compensation" accurately reflects trip-level labor remuneration.
4. **"Stores" vs. "Store Stops" vs. "Drop-offs" vs. "Stores & drop-offs"**
   - *Resolution:* Standardized to **Store Drop-offs** (plural) and **Store Drop-off** (singular). In multi-drop grocery logistics, a truck delivers to retail branch stores (e.g., "Landers Balintawak", "Kuya J Festival Mall"). "Store Drop-offs" captures both the client retail entity and the geographic stop action.
5. **"Net Operating Margin Equation" vs. "Net Profit Calculation"**
   - *Resolution:* Standardized to **Net Profit Calculation**. The calculation computes Net Profit ($\text{Revenue} - \text{Expenses}$), not the margin percentage.
